02 / Learn

Three ways a scam gets in.

Short modules, built from patterns that repeat across Malaysia. Each one ends with a specimen you can read like evidence.

  1. 01

    The login

    Phishing

    A stolen password is still the most common way an account is lost. The message borrows a bank’s colours, copies a login screen and adds a deadline — but the address is never quite right. AI has made the writing cleaner; it has not learned your bank’s real domain.

    1. The domain is a lookalike — one swapped letter, a hyphen, or .net instead of .com.my.
    2. A deadline measured in minutes, not days.
    3. You are asked to log in from a link, instead of opening the app you already have.

    The check Type the address yourself. Never log in from a link.

  2. 02

    The voice

    AI impersonation

    Cloning a voice takes a few seconds of audio — a story, a voicemail greeting, a video. Pair it with a new number and a family emergency, and the scam becomes a conversation you believe you are having with someone you trust. Video calls are the same trick with a face.

    1. A new number claims to be someone you know.
    2. The call cannot be returned, or the caller refuses to be seen.
    3. Money, gift cards or a transfer — urgently, and “don’t tell anyone yet”.

    The check Hang up. Call back on the number you already have.

  3. 03

    The offer

    Fake job offers

    The offer arrives on WhatsApp or Instagram: flexible hours, RM300 a day, no experience needed. The work is liking videos or “verifying orders”. Then the job asks you to pay a fee, or to receive money for someone else. That is the point at which it stops being a job.

    1. You are asked to pay to start work — a deposit, a training fee, an “activation”.
    2. The employer exists only on a messaging app, with no company name, address or contract.
    3. Your bank account is used to move someone else’s money. That makes you the mule.

    The check Never pay to start a job. Never lend your account to move money.

The Investigation

A tabletop detective game for groups of four. Each team gets a printed dossier — messages, timestamps, a bank statement, a voice-note transcript — and forty-five minutes to find the indicators of compromise, name the tactic, and say what the victim should have done at minute one.

About

AI-kido is a community workshop for secondary school students in Malaysia, run in partnership with schools outside Universiti Malaya. It was built for the room it is used in: a projector, cheap phones, and a class that has heard “be careful online” too many times to listen again.

The session has two halves. First, the three patterns above, taught through the messages themselves. Then the tabletop investigation, where teams apply the same checklist to a fictional case and defend their verdict.
